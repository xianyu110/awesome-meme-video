## What is the Ceiling Fan trend, and where did it start?

No single creator owns the fan transition. A CapCut "fan transition" outfit-change template was already going round TikTok on 26 August 2026, in a Spanish-language tutorial by @fingertips1246. The AI version took off a month later. On 24 September @shabastudio20 posted four outfits under a dark wooden fan with the line "Pick four outfits, add your photo ... and let the ceiling fan do the styling", and the clip passed 1.3 million plays.

Autumn "outfit rotation" clips followed within days, and The Tab published a how-to on 28 September. On 1 October one trend tracker counted 76 posts from 67 creators and about 3.9 million views, according to Kavel. The most-watched clip of all is a joke about the AI: on 3 October @amyrebeccaowen filmed a real attempt, captioned "trying the ceiling fan trend but I didn't realise people were using AI", in which she rolls off the sofa between outfits. It had 8.1 million plays by 8 October.

## How does the ceiling fan transition work?

The fan blade is the edit. In the real clips a phone is taped to the ceiling and looks straight down past a slowly turning fan at a person lying on a rug or a wooden floor. Each time a wide blade sweeps across the lens, it covers the person for a split second, and that is where the cut hides. When the blade has passed, the person is back in the same spot in a new outfit.

The clips we sampled show three to five changes in 13 to 16 seconds, with one blade pass every three seconds or so. The outfits contrast hard in color and style, and the shoes, bags and hats change too. That makes it a magic trick and an outfit inspiration post at the same time, which is why autumn "outfit rotation" clips took it over at the end of September.

The MakeViral version copies two viral AI reels of the trend, one with women's looks and one with men's looks. Your face, hair and skin tone come from your photo. The four outfits, the fan and the room come from the reel.
