## What is the Permission to Cry trend, and where did it start?

The meme grew out of the reaction account **@ghostreactings**, a man who reacts to videos dressed as a masked soldier from a video game, in a skull mask and a headset. On 4 September 2026 he posted "The Last Salute", a reaction to an AI-made clip of a salute at a military funeral. It reached 2.9 million views. He does not say the phrase in it, Know Your Meme notes, but his commenters kept asking whether they had permission to cry.

In late October the joke became its own meme. On 29 October @ada.gerry posted a "permission to cry sir." edit (599,700 views), and the same day @vexbolts posted a parody of the reactor in a helmet and a skull mask, ending on "Cry soldier, captain's orders" and "It's an order to cry" (1.8 million views). By 3 November the reactor joined in himself: "I grant you permission to cry with this video, soldiers," he says, and salutes (605,400 views).

Then AI versions arrived. On 4 November @hairless_gubby posted a white, furry AI creature that salutes at a flag-draped coffin and cries (351,000 views), and Know Your Meme added its "Permission to Cry, Soldier" entry the same day.
