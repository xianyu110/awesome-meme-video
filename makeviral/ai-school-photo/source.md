## What is the School Photo trend, and where did it start?

The trend took off on TikTok in late September 2026. On 24 September, mom of four Carlie Bond (@carlieb0nd) posted the school pictures she made at home: she sat her kids on a stool in front of a white wall, took the photos herself and asked ChatGPT to "keep them exactly the same but make it look like a school picture". The results came back on a cloudy blue backdrop. Her caption, "School pictures x3 kids?? In this economy?", had 914,600 plays by 8 October.

The AI school picture trend then split. A second wave of posts used AI to remove the watermark from photographers' proofs, and that is what made the news. USA Today covered the trend on 28 September and Fast Company on 29 September; by 7 October it had reached The Guardian, Dexerto and PetaPixel, and school photographers in the US and the UK had pushed back against the watermark removal. The idea of an AI school portrait is older: the 90s AI yearbook photo reached No. 1 on the US App Store in October 2023.

### Which photo works best?

Use **one sharp photo of your child from the chest up**, looking into the camera. The prompt keeps the face, the smile, the head angle and the clothes from your photo, and changes only the backdrop, the light and the crop. So the photo you take is the school photo you get.

- **Dress them for picture day.** The outfit stays as it is, so put on the shirt or sweater you want in the picture.
- **Stand them in front of a plain wall,** a step away from it, near a window in daylight. The viral originals were shot this way, on a stool against a white wall. A busy kitchen works too, but a plain wall gives the cleanest edges around the hair.
- **Hold the phone at their eye level,** about an arm's length away. From above, the head looks bigger and the chin smaller.
- **Turn off beauty mode and filters** in the camera app. The prompt keeps the skin as it is, so a smoothed photo stays smoothed.
- **Get the smile you want in the photo.** The AI does not change the expression, so a real laugh gives a real-laugh school photo.
- **One child per photo,** no hat and no sunglasses. Glasses are fine and stay on.
- **Use your own phone photo,** not a photographer's proof. A proof is the photographer's work, and the prompt keeps any watermark on it.
- **Only your own child,** or a child whose parent asked you to make it.

The usual problems are a child who looks a few years older, smooth plastic skin, perfect teeth where a front tooth is missing, or simply a different child with the same hair.

## AI school photo or the school photographer?

School photos are not cheap, which is why the trend took off. In September 2026, USA Today listed real US packages at $43, $60 and $65, and the school photo company Capturely puts most families' spend at $25 to $60 per child for a print package. In the UK, The Guardian quoted £20 for a single photo in October 2026. With three or four children, that adds up fast.

Still, the two do different jobs:

- **The school photographer's picture** is usually the one in the yearbook and on the student ID card, and each school sets those rules. An AI photo is school-style, not an official school photo, so ask your school before you send one in.
- **An AI school photo** is a keepsake: for the family chat, the grandparents, a frame on the shelf, or a picture day your child missed. It also helps when the official photo went wrong (eyes closed, a bad hair day) and retake day is weeks away.
- **Proofs stay the photographer's work.** Do not use AI, or any other tool, to remove the watermark from a proof you have not paid for: lawyers quoted by USA Today say it breaks copyright law. MakeViral does not offer it, and our prompt keeps any watermark that is already on a photo. Buy the print you love, or ask for a retake.

Many families do both: one small official package for school, and the extra looks, like the 90s laser backdrop, made at home.
