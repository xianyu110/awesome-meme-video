## What is the Pray For Me Slow Mo trend, and where did it start?

The **pray for me dance** began on 30 August 2020, when the dancer @lesgetitrell posted a slow-motion routine to a slowed version of "Pray For Me" by The Weeknd and Kendrick Lamar as a tribute to the late actor Chadwick Boseman. It reached 14.5 million plays, and the sound "pray for me slo mo" stayed in use for years.

In October 2026 the **pray for me tiktok** wave started. On 10 October @lesgetitrell posted a new take in a bright room (2.9 million plays), and groups of friends turned it into a slow mo run past the camera, one friend after another: @nadineeang's group on 14 October (5.5 million) and @kemanirae_'s group on 15 October (9.5 million), both crediting him for the dance. AI versions came within days: the first, on 13 October, put a new face into his room routine, and three-person AI templates followed on 21 October. By 25 October the sound had about 41,000 videos.

## The slow mo run trend, step by step

The slow mo run trend on TikTok has one simple rule: move as if the world is in slow motion. In the group version each friend starts far from the phone, runs toward it with wide, heavy arm swings and a serious face, and passes the lens on one side; the next friend follows. The joke is the drama: a hallway, a parking lot or a kitchen becomes an action film. The original pray for me choreography adds the prayer hands, the look up, the hand on the chest and the arms crossed over the chest at the end.

That is why people search for a pray for me slow mo dance tutorial: the real thing takes many takes ("after many retakes", "I fell doing this"). The AI version skips the retakes: you add photos, and the slow motion, the timing and the camera come out right the first time. Every run is a new take, so results differ.
