## What is the Shirt-to-Jacket Dance trend, and where did it start?

The clip is a real wedding video that has gone around Turkish social media for years. At night, in front of an apartment building, a man in a blue plaid shirt dances to the davul and zurna while a guest clicks wooden spoons beside his face. A few seconds in, his shirt seems to turn into a jacket: an optical illusion of the camera angle and the light, as Onedio explains it. On TikTok, @mesut0247 posted it on 18 June 2023 as "5 inci saniyeden sonra gömlek ceket oluyor" ("after the fifth second the shirt becomes a jacket"), and the tag #gömlekceketoluyor dates from May 2023. Reposts kept it alive, among them one by @6kingsam6 on 18 July 2026 with 1.1 million views.

In mid-September 2026 the AI versions took over. An AI edit that put a popular Turkish streamer into the dance passed 3.6 million views; others used footballers, politicians, old Yeşilçam film characters and, as Onedio noted, a giant cockroach. On 19 September @gkhngykc posted a version with his own face, made with Higgsfield's Genjutsu, and two days later a how-to video with his prompt that reached 1 million views. This template copies his reel.

## Why does the shirt turn into a jacket?

Nobody changes clothes. Onedio, which collected the AI versions on 25 September 2026, calls it an optical illusion: the camera angle and the light make the loose plaid shirt look like a jacket a few seconds after he starts to dance. Viewers replay the clip to catch the moment, and the 2023 caption that spread it says it happens "after the fifth second".

The AI versions keep the joke. Whoever dances, a footballer, a politician or a creator, the shirt still turns into a jacket. That is why the blue plaid shirt always stays in your video, even when your photo shows other clothes. @gkhngykc gave the same advice in his how-to: put the uncle's shirt on yourself first, so nobody says the AI changed the jacket. With one photo, MakeViral does that step for you.

**Where do the spoons come from?** In the clip, a guest clicks two wooden spoons like castanets. That is the sound of the spoon dance, kaşık oyunu, which the Turkish dance historian Metin And called "the most popular dance in Central Anatolia" in 1976. The clip itself is not a stage show, though: it is a man dancing at a wedding, with a guest who joins in with the spoons.
