## What is the Keep On Steppin trend, and where did it start?

The dance comes from Cale Brown, a dance creator with 10.7 million TikTok followers. On 31 October 2025 he posted a clip with four friends on an outdoor court next to an orchard, all in baggy jeans and black hoodies: the five step toward a high camera in a line, a camera on the ground catches their stomps and kicks, and each one takes a short solo. The sound was the southern soul track "Keep On Steppin". The post had 4.7 million views and 355,800 likes by 13 October 2026.

A year later the clip became an AI template. From 8 October 2026, UK CapCut template accounts posted versions that swap new people into the five-man line, most of them famous TV casts. The most viewed one, from 9 October, had 2.8 million views and 278,700 likes by 13 October. How-to videos followed the next day, the top one at 575,700 views, and from 10 October others showed the same swap in the Kling AI app.

### Which photo works best?

Upload **one full-body photo of you**, standing. You do the steps in your photo's own place.

- **Head to toes in view.** The dance is all footwork, so the feet must be in the photo. A waist-up photo gives stiff or made-up legs.
- **Standing, facing the camera**, arms relaxed, with some floor around you.
- **One person, nobody else.** Crop out friends, pets and people in the background.
- **Good light, no blur.** Sunglasses and a hood over the face make the face drift.
- **A vertical photo** gives a vertical clip. An outdoor court, a car park or a street looks closest to the original, and baggy jeans with a hoodie give the same look.
- Use a photo of yourself, or of someone who said yes.
