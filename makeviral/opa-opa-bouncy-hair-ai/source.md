## What is the Opa Opa Bouncy Hair trend, and where did it start?

The sound is a sped-up clip of "Ty zalip", a Russian dance track by PAPAKLEY and DJ Shiza. Its hook, "slysh, ty zalip? Opa, opa, op" (in Russian "слышь, ты залип? Опа, опа, оп"), means "hey, you zoned out? Opa, opa, op". The TikTok sound most clips use was posted on 12 August 2026 with a cosplay video.

The bounce took off on Russian-speaking TikTok in September: you hold the phone out for a selfie, bounce on the beat and let your hair fly. A clip from 18 September captioned "This trend is so addicting" had 14.2 million views and 2 million likes by 14 October, and @limonovai's selfie from 21 September had 2.6 million views. English posts call it the "opa opa opa trend" or the "Russian bounce hair trend", and write the hook in Latin letters as "slish ti zalip opa opa opa".

AI versions followed. App templates promised the bouncy hair from one photo, and anime edits of the bop spread, with tutorials from 3 and 5 October that shared their prompts. The trend is still growing: on 14 October about 65,800 TikTok videos used the main sound, about 400 more a day, twice the pace of the day before.
