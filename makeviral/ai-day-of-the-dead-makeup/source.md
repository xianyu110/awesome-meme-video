## What is the Catrina Makeup trend, and where did it start?

La Catrina is the elegant skeleton lady of Mexico's Day of the Dead. She began as a satirical etching by José Guadalupe Posada, first printed in 1913, and Diego Rivera put her into his 1946-47 mural *Dream of a Sunday Afternoon in the Alameda Park*. Painting your face as a Catrina, or as her partner the Catrín, is now a classic look for 1 and 2 November and for Halloween: in October 2025, US Google searches for "catrina makeup" reached about 60,500 and "day of the dead makeup" about 49,500.

The AI Day of the Dead photo took off in Mexico in October 2025, after Google's Gemini app learned to edit a photo and keep the face. Creators posted their Catrina prompts on TikTok, and on 9 and 11 October TV Azteca and Milenio printed step-by-step guides with a prompt to copy. In September 2026 it came back as CapCut AI templates, and on 8 October 2026 the Dreamina app added three Day of the Dead templates of its own.
