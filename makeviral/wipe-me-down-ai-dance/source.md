## What is the Wipe Me Down trend, and where did it start?

The dance is older than the AI trend. The Baltimore creator Carnell Nichols has danced his stomp to "Wipe Me Down", a 2007 rap song, on TikTok since at least June 2020, and he calls it the Carnell stomp. From 2023 his green-screen dance battles, where he out-dances people in other clips, took it to millions of views.

In summer 2026 the dance came back. On 17 August a creator from Myrtle Beach posted it in a kitchen with a friend: a lunge at the camera, a low bounce with the hands flicking at the floor, a jump, then the friend's turn. The clip had 12.4 million views and 1.8 million likes by 14 October. US Google searches for "wipe me down dance" rose from 590 in June to 1,300 in August.

From 28 September, CapCut AI templates turned one photo into the dance. A template post with a baby had 799,000 views and 24,900 shares by 14 October, and templates that swap new faces into the kitchen clip passed 650,000 views on 9 October.

### Which photo works best?

Upload **one full-body photo of you**, standing. You do the dance in your photo's own place.

- **Head to toes in view.** The dance lives in the knees and feet, so the feet must be in the photo. A waist-up photo gives stiff or made-up legs.
- **Standing, facing the camera**, arms relaxed, with some floor around you and some space above your head for the hops.
- **One person, nobody else.** Crop out friends, pets and people in the background.
- **Good light, no blur.** Sunglasses, a hood or a cap low over the eyes make the face drift.
- **A vertical photo** gives a vertical clip. A kitchen or a living room with open floor looks closest to the viral clips.
- Use a photo of yourself, or of an adult who said yes.
