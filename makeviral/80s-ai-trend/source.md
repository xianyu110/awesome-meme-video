### Which photo works best?

Use **one clear, front-facing selfie** with your whole face in good light. The prompt keeps your face and changes everything else, so the face is the only thing that must be right.

- Head and shoulders is enough. The model invents the 80s outfit and the room.
- Take off sunglasses and hats; they hide the features the model needs to keep.
- Avoid heavy beauty filters. A filtered face comes back as someone slightly different.
- One person per photo. With two faces, the model has to guess which one to keep.
- Use a photo of yourself or of an adult who agreed. Never a celebrity or a stranger.

## What makes a 1985 photo look real?

The viral versions work because they copy the flaws of cheap 1980s cameras, not just the fashion. Our photo prompt asks for each of these on purpose:

| Detail | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Direct flash | Flat, bright faces and hard shadows behind the subject, like a compact camera from the era. |
| Faded warm color and grain | Old print film shifts toward orange and loses contrast over the years. |
| Red-orange date stamp | The detail most people remember from family albums. It tells the viewer "1985" at a glance. |
| Wood panels, boxy TV, cassette player | A period room makes the outfit believable. |
| No modern objects | One phone or flat screen breaks the effect. |

The video prompt does the same for motion: low resolution, color bleed, tracking lines along the bottom edge, handheld shake and autofocus that hunts.

## What goes wrong, and how do you fix it?

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| The face looks like a different person | Use a sharper, front-facing selfie with no filter, then generate the still again. |
| The photo looks like a modern costume party | Generate again. The grain, the flash and the date stamp should be strong; if they are weak, the image reads as 2026. |
| A phone or a modern logo shows up | Generate the still again before you animate it. The video model keeps what is in the first frame. |
| The date stamp shows the wrong year or random letters | AI text is often wrong. Crop it out, or keep it; viewers rarely read it closely. |
| The bike or hands warp in the video | Common in 10-second clips. A second run, or a shorter clip, often fixes it. |
| The video model refuses the photo | Some models refuse some real faces. The credits come back; try Veo 3.1 Fast. |
