## What is the Whoville trend, and where did it start?

The Whos of Whoville come from Dr. Seuss's 1957 book How the Grinch Stole Christmas!, and their tall, swirly hair became famous with the 2000 live-action film, whose makeup won an Academy Award. Every December since, people search for the look: in December 2025 US Google searches for "whoville character" reached 110,000 a month and "whoville hair" 40,500, for Christmas parties, costumes and hair days.

The AI version started on TikTok on 13 November 2026, when @ad_1981 posted a slideshow titled "Ask ChatGPT which Whoville are you from", with her own ChatGPT portrait and a long prompt in the caption under the words "COPY & PASTE THIS PROMPT WITH YOUR PHOTO". On 14 and 15 November at least nine more accounts posted the same prompt word for word, each with their own Whoville transformation, and on 16 November the first tutorials appeared. The posts are still small (each under 1,500 views on 16 November), but the Whoville trend hashtag #whovilletrend already holds 3.4 million views from earlier Christmas seasons, and searches for "whoville character" stood at 40,500 in October 2026, before the season.

### Which photo works best?

Use **one sharp selfie of one adult**, from the chest up, looking into the camera. The AI keeps your face, smile, eye color, skin tone and hair color, and changes the hairstyle, the outfit and the background.

- **Any hair length works:** it becomes tall curls in your own color.
- **No hat and no sunglasses.** Glasses are fine.
- **Duo and Trio:** one photo per person, in left-to-right order.
- **Pet versions:** one pet per photo, the face, both eyes and the ears in view.
- **Adults and pets only.** Never upload a child's photo, and only use photos of people who said yes.

## The ChatGPT Whoville prompt, and what we changed

We use the prompt from the trend's first post, the one every later post copied, in its own words and order: "Using my uploaded photo as the reference, transform me into an adorable, whimsical resident of ...", the "keep me recognizable" rules, the makeover (an extravagant, gravity-defying Christmas hairstyle with playful curls, festive bows and candy-cane decorations, a slightly whimsical nose, rosy cheeks, a luxurious outfit with fluffy fur trim, scarves and bells), the background of colorful, crooked Christmas houses with glowing windows and falling snow, and the cinematic, live-action portrait style.

What we changed, and why:

- **The names.** The book, the film, the town and its sign are trademarks, so our prompt describes the look in words ("a snowy Christmas storybook town") instead of naming them, and the sign says "Merry Christmas". The look is the same.
- **The nose.** In the trend's best results the nose keeps its shape and only gets a rosy tip. A longer or pointed nose is the fastest way to lose the likeness, so our prompt asks for your own nose with a soft red tip.
- **Your face.** Our identity rules go at the end of every prompt: the face shape, eyes, eye color, skin tone, age and smile stay yours, and only the hair, the outfit and the background change.

You can copy the prompt from the create page and edit it, but the one-tap flow runs it for you.
