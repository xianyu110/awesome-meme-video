## What is the What If You Fly trend, and where did it start?

The words are older than the video. "What if I fall? Oh but my darling, what if you fly?" is a line by the poet Erin Hanson, and "but what if you fly" has been a motivation caption on TikTok for years, under hashtags like #whatifyoufly.

On 6 November 2026 the AI filmmaker Jonnel Cayabyab (@jnl.creatives), based in Bali, posted a 17-second AI clip of himself that acts the line out. He sits at his desk late at night, filmed by the webcam: "What if you fall?" as his chair tips back, "Twin!" with both hands on his head, then "What if you..." and a giant "FLY". The floor drops away, he falls into the morning sky, fire roars from his hands and feet, and he flies low over the sea and up above the clouds, to a soaring film-score track. The words he says are printed on his T-shirt. The same day he shared every prompt behind it, including a ready prompt that puts your own selfie into his finished video. That is how the What If You Fly AI trend began, and his prompt is the one this template starts from.

By 16 November his post had 339,200 views and 59,300 likes. On 8 November the Canadian twins @vorostwins posted a parody that starts with his clip and answers it with "You stop using generative AI"; it passed 1.1 million views. Copies on his sound followed, with about 30 new videos a day by 16 November.
