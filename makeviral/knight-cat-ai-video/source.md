## What is the Knight Cat trend, and where did it start?

The knight cat comes from one AI creator, **@sickmanai**, who calls the character "Kitty". On 19 August 2026 the creator posted part 1 of the "Kitten vs Dragon" series on Instagram: a fluffy kitten in silver armor and a pink bow sips an iced drink, then sets off to fight a dragon. Part 2 followed on 21 August. The kitten stands in a storm on a foggy field, gripping a sword taller than itself, and that frame became the meme. By 7 October the two reels had 478,000 and 970,000 likes.

Five weeks later it broke out in two languages within days of each other. On X, a post from 27 September captioned "me trying to handle things maturely" reached 794,800 views. Russian speakers across the CIS call it "котенок в доспехах", the kitten in armor, or the kitten with a sword; Russian clips and searches started on 29 September and kept rising until 5 October. On 28 September @sickmanai and the AI gym-bro account @derek.mercer.2006 posted "Remember your gym bro.", a giant knight beside the tiny one. In early October people started to put themselves, their pets and their children into the scene.

### Which photo works best?

Use **one sharp photo of your pet with the whole body in view**, ears to paws. The prompt stands the animal upright in armor, so the model needs to see the legs, the fur color and the markings.

- Standing or sitting, fully visible and in focus, in good daylight. A curled-up sleeper or a blurry action shot gives the model too little.
- Shoot at your pet's eye level if you can.
- One animal per photo. With two, the model may merge them.
- Any background works: the prompt replaces it with the misty moor.
- For **You and your pet** and **Sleeping dragon**, add your photo **first** (full or half body, face clear), then your pet. The first photo becomes the big knight.
- Use your own pet, and your own face or that of an adult who agreed. Never a celebrity.

## What goes wrong with a knight cat video, and how do you fix it?

We tested these prompts on a cat and a dog with both video models before we published them. These are the real problems we hit:

| What goes wrong | What the prompt does about it | If you still see it |
|---|---|---|
| The blade covers your pet's face | Holds the sword upright "just beside its face, so the blade never hides the face" | Use "Edit the prompts yourself" to make the still first, and animate the one you like. |
| The paws look fused with the sword grip | Puts both front paws on the hilt at chest height | A known small flaw at this size. Each run is different, so a second run can fix it. |
| A dog gets steel fingers instead of paws | Keeps the breed and body from your photo | Happens with some dogs; cats keep their furry paws. Run it again, or try adding "furry paws, no fingers" to the photo prompt. |
| The armor leaves the belly bare | Asks for a full plate suit with a chainmail skirt, leg plates and small steel boots | One of our dog tests had this. The full suit fixed it. |
| The camera pulls back and your pet shrinks to a dot | Keeps the camera "low and close" with a very slow push in | Our first prompt pulled back on both models. This line fixed it. |
| You and your pet turn your backs to the camera | Turns both heads to the left in three-quarter view, "never turned away from the camera" | Our early tests did this. The final prompt fixed it on both models. |
| Cuts, thunder or smoke appear | Asks for "one continuous shot with no cuts" and "no smoke, no fire" | Kling 3.0 (the Standard option) adds more by itself; in one test it added lightning. Pick Best for one clean take. |
| The clip has music but hardly any wind | Asks for howling wind "from the first second" | Seedance 2.5 (Best) follows it. Kling 3.0 (Standard) left the wind almost silent in all four of our comparison clips. |
