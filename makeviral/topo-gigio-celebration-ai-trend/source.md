## What is the Topo Gigio Celebration trend, and where did it start?

The gesture is older than the trend. On 8 April 2001, in a Boca Juniors vs River Plate derby, Juan Román Riquelme scored and stood still with his hands cupped behind his ears, as if he could not hear the crowd. People named it after Topo Gigio, the big-eared puppet mouse from Italian TV, and Riquelme later said it was for his daughter, who loved the character. Since then the Riquelme Topo Gigio celebration has been copied all over Argentine football.

On 15 July 2026, in the World Cup semi-final in Atlanta, Enzo Fernández scored Argentina's 85th-minute equaliser against England with a strike from outside the box and did the same celebration. Argentina won 2-1. The Enzo Fernandez celebration vs England became a meme, and a photo of it posted by his club in England caused a small controversy among English fans.

In October it turned into an AI trend. On 12 October 2026 @ns7films posted an AI clip that put a fantasy TV character in his place; it passed 430,000 views. #topogigiotrend, started on 15 October, had 685 videos and nearly 2 million views a day later. On 18 October the Instagram creator @nekit_rkv posted the Shrek celebration: the green ogre salutes on the touchline and cups his ears at the crowd. It had 238,000 likes a day later, and fans called it "Shrek Fernandez".

## Topo Gigio Celebration trend questions

### What is the Topo Gigio celebration meaning?

Both hands cupped behind the ears means "I can't hear you, louder": a player says it to the crowd, or to critics, after a goal. The name comes from Topo Gigio, the puppet mouse with big ears. The Enzo Fernandez celebration meaning is the same: Enzo Fernández did it after his equaliser against England.

### What was the Enzo celebration vs England?

On 15 July 2026 Enzo Fernández scored Argentina's 85th-minute equaliser in the World Cup semi-final against England in Atlanta. He put his hands behind his ears, puffed out his cheeks and pushed his lips forward as if to blow a kiss to the stands. Argentina won 2-1, and videos with the Enzo Fernandez celebration explained frame by frame spread on social media.

### Is it the same as the Riquelme celebration?

Yes. Juan Román Riquelme first did the Topo Gigio celebration for Boca Juniors against River Plate on 8 April 2001, and it became his trademark.
