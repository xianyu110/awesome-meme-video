### Which photo works best?

Use a **sharp, front-facing photo of your face** with both eyes open and visible. The effect lives in the eyes, so they need detail.

- A head-and-shoulders selfie works best. The photo step rebuilds the night scene, the light and the person in front of you.
- Start from a soft smile or a neutral look. The clip opens on a fragile smile.
- Even light on the face. Heavy shadows or sunglasses hide the eyes and the tears.
- Any background is fine: the photo step replaces it with the night park.
- Use a photo of yourself or of an adult who agreed to it. Never a stranger, a celebrity or a child.

## Why do people post AI crying videos?

Mostly for the drama, and often as a joke. The clip is ten seconds of a movie-level goodbye on a face everyone knows, so it works as a reaction: "me when the group chat leaves me on read", "me after one sad song", "when my favourite cafe closes". The mismatch between a small everyday caption and a K-drama farewell is the punchline. One Turkish caption in the trend sums it up: "I wish I cried this beautifully in real life".

It also works played straight. Creators pair the scene with a line about a breakup, a move or missing home, and viewers answer in the comments. The format asks for nothing but your face, which is why it crossed languages so fast.

## What goes wrong with the AI crying face, and how do you fix it?

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| The face changes into someone else | Use a sharper, larger photo of the face. Keep "exactly the same" in the prompt. |
| The crying looks cartoonish or loud | Keep "real, subtle acting, never exaggerated" in the prompt. A second run often comes back calmer. |
| The person in front is too sharp | Keep "heavily blurred into a soft shape" in the photo prompt, or run the photo step again. |
| Makeup, a hijab or glasses shift | Name them in the prompt: "keep the pink hijab and the round glasses". |

## Is it okay to make someone cry with AI?

Keep it light and kind. The AI crying effect is a dramatic scene, and the best posts are obviously playful. Make it with your own face or with an adult who said yes and knows where it will be posted.

Do not use it to fake someone's distress: no clips that pretend a real person broke down, no "proof" in an argument, no messages meant to guilt or scare someone. That can hurt people and break platform rules. When the clip could be taken as real, say it is AI in the caption. Many posting apps also offer an AI-generated label.
