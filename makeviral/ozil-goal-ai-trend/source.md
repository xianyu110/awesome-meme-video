## What is the Ozil Goal trend, and where did it start?

The goal is real. On 1 November 2016, in a Champions League group game in Sofia, Mesut Özil scored Arsenal's winner against Ludogorets two minutes from time to make it 3-2. He ran onto a long ball, flicked it past the goalkeeper as he came out, sent two defenders sliding to the ground and rolled the ball into the empty net.

Nearly ten years later, AI video tools turned it into a meme. By 9 October 2026, TikTok creators were posting AI copies of the clip with someone else as the scorer, and a how-to video that day showed the goal recast with Higgsfield's Genjutsu. That evening @r7538701fal posted a version with a blocky video game villager as the scorer, set to a French love song. By 11 October it had 1.9 million views, 224,200 likes and 76,100 shares, and its sound was on 304 videos. Versions with grandmas and TV characters spread too, and dozens of how-to videos followed.

On 10 October the same account posted a version with an AI-made grandma as the scorer, in made-up kits with no real club badges. MakeViral copies that reel: your photo takes her place.

You need **one photo of the scorer**: you, a friend who agreed, or your grandma with her OK.

- Face sharp and turned to the camera, with no sunglasses. The face is small in most shots, so a clear photo matters.
- One person in the photo, with nothing in front of them.
- Use a photo of yourself or of an adult who said yes. Never a footballer, a celebrity or a stranger.
