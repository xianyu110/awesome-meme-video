## What is the Kolpaçino Car Scene trend, and where did it start?

The line comes from the 2011 Turkish comedy Kolpaçino: Bomba. In the back seat of a car, the character Ganyotçu gets cut off on the phone, turns to the man next to him and lets it all out: "Abi, bak sen bana imkân vermiyorsun! Dayımın çocukları cinayetten yeni çıktılar!" ("Look, brother, you're not giving me a chance! My cousins just got out for murder!"). He builds up to the punchline, "Benim dayımın çocukları var ya, mermiye kafa atıyorlar!": "My cousins, you know, they headbutt bullets!" The man beside him barely reacts.

The scene went up on YouTube as early as March 2013, and a 2020 upload by Film Adresi has 1.27 million views. TikTok reposts keep it alive: one from July 2025 has 345,000 views, and the #kolpaçino tag counts 2.2 billion views. In late October 2026 the scene turned into an AI trend: from 26 October an AI video app ran Turkish ads for a two-photo template that puts two users in the back seat at night. ## What does "mermiye kafa atıyor" mean?

Word for word, "mermiye kafa atıyor" means "he headbutts bullets": someone so tough that a bullet is just a ball to head. So "benim dayımın çocukları mermiye kafa atıyor" is "my cousins (my uncle's sons) headbutt bullets", the most over-the-top threat a man can make when he has nothing else. That is the answer to mermiye kafa atmak ne demek: it is a joke about a fake tough guy, not a real threat.

The joke works because of the build-up and the other man. Ganyotçu goes from "sen bana imkân vermiyorsun" to dayımın çocukları cinayetten yeni çıktı to the bullets, louder every line, and the man beside him does not move. That is why the quote is everywhere in Turkish group chats, and why people remember it in many forms: dayımın çocukları mermiye kafa atıyorlar, mermiye kafa atıyorlar, dayımın oğulları mermiye kafa atıyor, or even benim amcamın çocukları mermiye kafa atıyor. Typed without Turkish letters it becomes dayimin cocuklari mermiye kafa atiyor.
