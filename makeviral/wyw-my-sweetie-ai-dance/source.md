## What is the Wyw My Sweetie Dance trend, and where did it start?

The song is "Wyw my sweetie #JerseyClub" by nissi & 25Hzz. "Wyw" stands for "where you at": the hook goes "my sweet, where you at", and a slowed version went up as a TikTok sound on 27 August 2026. On 17 September the Japanese choreographer @akanenmiyoshi posted her own routine to it with the caption "my sweet WYA?" (where you at). That clip passed 3.4 million views, and within days creators everywhere were learning it.

The trend grew in two shapes. Solo dancers post the routine as it is. Duo posts turn it into a small skit: one dancer does the steps alone, then reaches out of the frame and pulls a partner, a crush or a husband in for the end. The biggest post so far, a duo take from the US on 8 October, passed 25 million views, and K-pop idols and big creators posted their own versions in October. On TikTok it runs under #wywmysweetie (2,270 videos and 37.3 million views on 24 October, still about 100 new videos a day) and #mysweetie, and many people call it simply the come back and back trend.

Then came the AI twist. From 13 October, viral posts claimed the song itself is AI-made; one, "this viral tiktok song is actually AI", had 4.76 million views by 23 October, and Google now suggests "is www my sweetie an ai song". Dancers answered with "non AI" versions: a German dancer's "my sweetie trend, but with the non AI version" Short passed 1 million views on YouTube within two days of 21 October. Whether the vocals are really AI is not confirmed. Meanwhile AI dancer accounts post AI-made dancers doing the routine, and that is what this template makes with your photo.
