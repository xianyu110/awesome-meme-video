### Which photo works best?

Use a **sharp, front-facing photo** with your face and upper body in view. The prompt dresses you in white and puts you on stone steps, so your clothes and background do not matter much. Your face does: the closer and sharper it is in the photo, the more the video looks like you.

- Look straight into the camera with a neutral or calm face. The whole joke is that you do not react, so a big smile works against it.
- Good light on the face, no sunglasses, no hat that hides the eyes.
- One person in the photo. A group shot confuses which face should lead the crowd.
- Skip photos with a cigarette, a drink or a logo in view. The prompt bans them, and a clean start gives a cleaner result.
- Use a photo of yourself or of an adult who agreed. Never a celebrity or a stranger.

## Why is the Storm crowd video so popular?

It is pure contrast. Everyone in the frame moves at once, hard and fast, and the one person in the center does nothing. That stillness reads as power, and it lets anyone play the lead: a student, a boss, a grandmother, a baby. One of the early viral copies on 13 September was a baby version that passed 315,300 views.

It also rewards a second watch. The bow lands on the beat, the lead stays calm, and the viewer loops it to see the crowd drop again.

The search data shows how people look for it. Most people know it by the artist and the song, not by a trend name.
